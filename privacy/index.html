Earthquake Atlas privacy policy

Earthquake Atlas has no user accounts. The app stores preferences and some recent earthquake data on your device so it can restore your settings and show cached information when you are offline.

Location and notifications

If you grant location permission, Earthquake Atlas uses your location to show nearby earthquakes and other local information. If you enable nearby notifications, the app sends the alert service your approximate location, rounded to one decimal place. Alert registration also sends an Apple push token, your selected alert locations and names, magnitude and distance thresholds, notification language, and quiet hours and time-zone offset where applicable. We use this information to select and deliver the alerts you request.

When you follow an earthquake sequence as a Live Activity, the alert service stores its Apple update token and the public earthquake information needed to refresh it. These records normally expire within an hour after the 12-hour follow period. Alert registrations expire after 180 days unless refreshed. Turning alerts off in the app sends a request to remove that registration. Unfollowing a sequence sends a request to remove its update token.

The alert service uses Cloudflare to store registration records and Apple Push Notification service to deliver notifications. You can change notification and location permissions in Settings on your device.

Other services

Earthquake Atlas requests public earthquake, terrain, imagery, and waveform data from external providers. These providers receive the requests needed to return the content. Place search uses Apple Maps. When you open an external link, including a USGS reporting form or an Amazon product search, the destination handles your visit and any information you submit under its own privacy policy. Earthquake Atlas may earn a commission from qualifying Amazon purchases made through affiliate links.
